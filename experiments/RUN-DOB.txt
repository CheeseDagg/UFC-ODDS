The block is a SHA-256 proof of work:

    nonce  = a fresh 16-hex string, per response
    target = "0" * 2, the difficulty, stated in the page
    find the smallest n where sha256(f"{nonce}:{n}") starts with target
    POST nonce=<nonce>&n=<n>, form-encoded, to /__c  -> sets the cookie
    re-request the original URL

At difficulty 2 that is about 256 hashes. Microseconds in hashlib. The block
was never about compute — it was about being a browser, and the old client
failed it three ways at once: it announced itself as a bot, it threw away the
cookie the toll buys, and it had no way to run the arithmetic.

Round 7 pays the toll once on the events page and rides the cookie through the
26 index pages and ~1,270 detail pages. Nothing is hard-coded: the nonce, the
difficulty and the POST path are all parsed out of the page, because the nonce
is per-response and the difficulty is the site's to raise. If the shape ever
changes, the solver returns None and the run reports "challenge I could not
solve" — a legible failure instead of another silent zero.

The selftest runs the fake site BEHIND the challenge, so it exercises the
grind, the POST and the retry, and asserts the toll is paid exactly once
across 1,300 requests rather than once per request.
